Stricter Visa Rules Drive International Students Away from U.S. Universities

Universities across the country face a lasting enrollment downturn as prospective students look abroad—and those who do arrive encounter new hurdles settling into American life.

MN
By Mainak NairPublished September 14, 2034 · New York

NEW YORK, 2034U.S. universities are seeing a sustained decline in international student enrollment as stricter visa rules, longer processing times, and tighter post-graduation work policies push more students toward Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, and other destinations. Universities are adapting their recruitment and support strategies as fewer international students choose to study in the United States.

For those who do arrive, adjusting to life in the U.S. has become more complicated. Students often need to navigate changing immigration requirements while also establishing housing, banking, employment, and other essential services with limited local history or documentation. Universities and community organizations are responding by expanding pre-arrival guidance and newcomer support, while financial institutions face growing pressure to make their requirements clearer and more accessible to people entering the U.S. financial system for the first time.
